# Build it with an AI

Paste these turns one at a time into a chat with an AI connected to 3DCraft (Help › Connect an AI agent).
Each turn says what you want, the way you would tell a colleague; the AI picks the commands.

## The conversation

1. Let's design an original stovetop moka pot in cast aluminium, twelve-sided, for 3, 4 or 6 cups, sized by
   the physics of brewing and meeting EN 13248. Put the sizes in a table: the cups; the gasket's inner and
   outer diameter (50 × 65 mm for 3 and 4 cups, 55 × 71 for 6); the joint thread, ISO 261 with a 2 mm fine
   pitch (M68 for 3 cups, M72 for 4, M80 for 6); and the base across its flats (90, 95 and 105 mm). Make the
   cups a choice from the table, 3 by default, and the handle's lean from the vertical a second parameter,
   0° to 30° in 5° steps, 15° by default. A cup is 50 mL of water; the coffee is 1 part to 10 of water by
   mass, and loose ground coffee weighs 0.33 g/mL.

2. The boiler is a twelve-sided frustum, one flat facing +x, narrowing upward by 7° (drafted, for a die drawn
   upward), with a 4 mm base, a flat shoulder on top and a round collar of the thread's diameter, 13 mm tall,
   with a 10 mm external thread. Round its vertical, base and shoulder edges 3 mm. Its cavity comes from a
   sand core: make that core as its own body, kept and hidden so you can measure it: the frustum 3 mm inside
   the outside, from the floor to 3 mm under the shoulder, rounded 3 mm on all its edges, with the neck on top,
   a bore 2.5 mm inside the thread's root, up through the collar. The fill line lies 3 mm below the bottom of
   the funnel's cone (next turn), or lower if the valve boss would otherwise come within 3 mm of the
   shoulder's round; the floor goes where the cavity holds the cups' water below the fill line, besides what
   the funnel's tube takes. On the facet facing −y put the safety valve: a boss Ø 15 mm, 2.5 mm proud and
   square to the facet, drafted 1.5° and rooted with a 1 mm round, tapped M10 × 1 with the lowest point of
   its Ø 9 mm tapping hole on the fill line, and a brass valve screwed in: 4 mm of thread, a Ø 12 × 3 mm
   flange, a Ø 8 × 2 mm cap and a Ø 3 × 2.5 mm plunger, edges broken 0.5 mm.

3. The funnel is pressed from 0.8 mm aluminium. Its flange lies on the boiler's rim and reaches to 0.5 mm
   inside the collector's tap bore; its basket clears the neck's bore by 0.6 mm and is just deep enough to
   hold the coffee levelled at its rim, with a 2 mm round in the inner floor corner. Under the floor a 15°
   cone leads into a tube of 0.2 times the basket's radius that ends 3 mm above the boiler's floor; round the
   wall-to-cone and cone-to-tube bends 2 mm too. Perforate the floor with Ø 1.5 mm holes in a hexagonal
   lattice of five rings whose outermost reaches 4 mm inside the wall. On the flange goes the gasket, 4 mm
   silicone, and on that the filter plate, 1 mm aluminium of the gasket's outer diameter, with Ø 1 mm holes in
   a hexagonal lattice of eight rings reaching 1.5 mm inside the gasket's bore.

4. The collector is twelve-sided too, aligned with the boiler. Its skirt starts 1.5 mm above the boiler's
   shoulder with its flats 3 mm outside the thread's crest, and it flares 5° upward; round its vertical edges
   4 mm. The base is 4 mm thick above the filter plate. Make the chamber as a core: 5° facets rounded 3 mm,
   kept and hidden, with a central column Ø 12 mm narrowing 1.5° upward and a 3 mm round at its foot, as tall
   as holds 1.1 times the water, its top rounded 1 mm, and 12 mm of chamber above it. Turn the skirt to the
   tap drill with the internal thread, a seat for the gasket and the plate, a 1.5 mm conical recess under the
   base from the gasket's bore, and a Ø 5.5 mm bore up the column. Give it a V spout on the +x facet: a ruled
   loft from an 18 mm wide, 10 mm deep triangle 2 mm inside the facet and 1 mm under the rim, out to the same
   triangle at 0.3 of its size 12 mm beyond the facet and 4 mm higher; a channel of the same shape, raised
   by a 2 mm wall and starting 8 mm inside the facet, cuts through the rim, and the spout's keel and root are
   rounded 2 mm. On the −x facet a lug for the handle and the lid: 14 mm wide, 16 mm deep, flush with the rim,
   22 mm beyond the facet, sides drafted 1.5°, rounded 3 mm with its root. A Ø 3 mm pin crosses it 3 mm below
   the rim and 7 mm out from the facet, in a 5 mm slot that reaches 0.5 mm beyond a 6 mm circle about the pin.

5. The lid starts with a 3 mm band, overhanging the rim by 0.5 mm and drafted 1.5°, and rises 10 mm as a low
   twelve-sided pyramid to an outline 0.42 times its size: a ruled loft through the three outlines, its
   ridges, plateau edges and band corners rounded 4 mm, shelled 3 mm and open below. Its hinge ear is a 4 mm
   plate from inside the band out over the lug, its top level with the band's top and its outer edge an arc
   of 6 mm about the pin, so the lid can turn. A black phenolic knob sits on the plateau: Ø 20 × 12 mm, a
   Ø 14 mm neck 4 mm tall, a 45° flare and a crown rounded 3 mm. The handle is black phenolic: a
   20 × 18.5 mm rounded section (5 mm corners) whose top is 0.5 mm under the rim, starting 0.5 mm beyond the
   slot over the lug, running out 13 mm, bending down round 12 mm, then a 70 mm grip at the handle angle;
   round the grip's end 5 mm and cut its socket over the lug.

6. Make the castings and the sheet parts aluminium, the valve brass, the pin steel, the gasket silicone and the
   handle and knob phenolic, with their densities. Add measured outputs for the water below the valve, the
   basket's capacity, the collector's capacity below the column's mouth, the mass and the centre of mass, and
   check: the water equals the cups' within 0.5 %, the basket holds the dose within 0.5 %, the collector
   holds at least the water, the funnel ends at least 2 mm above the floor, the pot does not tip toward its
   handle on a 10° slope empty or brewed (all the water in the collector), every die-formed face drafted at
   least 1° toward its die, the lid turned 90° about its pin clear of the collector and the handle, the
   handle at least 30 mm from the boiler, the valve at least 2 mm from the collector, the hole counts, and no
   two parts colliding except the two screwed pairs. Give the document a title and a description, check the
   whole document, and show me a picture from the front right, a little above the pot.

## Acceptance

At 3 cups and 15° the result must have:

- ten parts (boiler, safety valve, funnel, gasket, filter plate, collector, lid, pin, knob, handle) and the
  two hidden cores;
- 150 mL of water below the safety valve within 0.5 %, measured on the cavity less the funnel;
- a basket of Ø 57.75 mm inside and 17.41 mm deep, holding 45.45 mL within 1 % (15 g of coffee);
- a collector holding at least 150 mL below the column's mouth (165 mL within 2 %);
- the boiler's facets drafted 7°, the collector's 5°, the column, the lug's sides and the lid's band 1.5°;
- 217 holes in the filter plate and 91 in the funnel's floor;
- a base of 90 mm across flats and an overall height of 150.1 mm to the top of the knob, within 1 %;
- a tipping angle toward the handle of at least 10° empty and brewed (about 27° and 26°);
- a lid that turns 90° about its pin without touching the collector or the handle;
- the handle at least 30 mm from the boiler (about 46 mm), and no collisions but the screwed pairs;
- every check passing, and the same at 4 and 6 cups and at handle angles of 0° and 30°.
